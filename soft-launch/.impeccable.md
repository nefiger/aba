## Design Context

Canonical public delivery contract: [`PUBLIC-VOICE-AND-DESIGN-GUARDRAILS.md`](PUBLIC-VOICE-AND-DESIGN-GUARDRAILS.md). Read and apply it before any public copy or page-design change.

Last confirmed: 2026-07-20

### Users

The public experience serves locally rooted African biologicals manufacturers, distributors, formulators, technical experts, policy and institutional stakeholders, people with real registration situations, and prospective future members. Founding members use the member application first; later cohorts and, when ABA is ready, public applicants use that same page. ABA operators must be able to turn every capture into coherent person, organisation, application, membership, consent, and follow-up records.

Visitors often arrive from a shared link with little context, on phone or desktop, and without anyone available to narrate the site. They need to understand what ABA is, why it matters, what membership offers, which action is appropriate, and what happens after sharing information.

### Brand Personality

Rooted, authoritative, catalytic.

The experience should create confidence that ABA is serious, African-led, technically credible, useful, and gathering real momentum. It must be constructive and forward-looking without sounding inflated, hostile, corporate-slick, or like a generic NGO or software platform.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use the final ABA brochure as the primary creative reference for the whole site and the approved logo at `design/assets/aba-logo-final.png` as the immutable mark. The visual direction is a contemporary African institution with technical field-guide clarity: light-first warm reading surfaces, decisive deep-green fields, calm sage structure, and rare burnt-orange emphasis drawn from the brochure.

Use the exact logo colours as the palette foundation and the brochure's leaf green, warm neutral and orange as controlled companions. Do not inherit the old prototype token system as design authority. Translate the brochure through full-bleed agricultural imagery, hard colour fields, compact uppercase labels, disciplined rules, structured information blocks, inset statements, uneven panel groupings, outcome bands and purposeful orange interruptions. Use Archivo for body and operating information, Saira Semi Condensed for concise page identity, and Lora selectively for chapter statements and proposition language. The serif is a shared type role, not a page-specific flourish and not permission for magazine-like dominance.

The shared CSS foundation owns the palette, type roles, labels, chapter bands, image fields, callouts, rules, controls, forms and footer. Individual routes compose those shared elements differently according to their job; they do not create independent visual systems.

Avoid generic SaaS card grids, endless rounded panels, glass effects, decorative metrics, tiny symbols in white blobs, random cropped symbol fragments, and an interface that looks patched onto the current monorepo frontend.

### Design Principles

1. Lead with institutional purpose and membership meaning, not software features or forms.
2. Treat the membership value proposition as one connected system of value, not a ranked benefit list.
3. Make every route and form consequence explicit while keeping internal rollout narration out of public copy.
4. Use disciplined hierarchy, negative space, structured information blocks, and a small number of decisive colour fields instead of repetitive cards or editorial spreads.
5. Design the shared data model through one coherent member application: founders, later invited cohorts and eventual public applicants complete the same page and questions. Cohort or invitation provenance is internal source metadata, not a different visible form.
6. Use colour accessibly: deep green carries text and primary structure; sage supports; brochure orange is rare emphasis, with a deeper orange for accessible text/actions.
7. Preserve the distinction between membership, membership interest, Technical Network application, tracker participation, and communication consent.
8. Design mobile as a real reading and form context, not a reduced desktop layout.
9. Every hero title must remain on one visual line at every supported viewport. Achieve this with concise copy, responsive typography, and sufficient layout width—never overflow, clipping, truncation, or an illegibly small fallback.
10. Consistency must come from the brand system, not repeated page silhouettes. Do not propagate oversized condensed headings, full-width colour slabs, or identical section rhythms across routes; vary density, type roles, composition, and pacing according to each page's job.
11. Let the brochure lead the public voice as well as the visual tone. Use active, declarative language that names what ABA is building, where it is building it, and who it is for. Avoid twee slogan fragments, passive constructions such as `X, organised`, abstract paired nouns, the word `ambition`, and internal system-model terms such as `route`, `relationship`, `context`, `record`, or `operating system` in public copy.
12. Do not manufacture rhythm by breaking one thought into stacked sentence fragments such as `Useful work. Honest limits.` or `Africa-wide ambition. Starting in South Africa.` Headings must express a complete idea, and desktop headings must not wrap simply to create visual drama.
