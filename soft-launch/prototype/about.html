About the alliance

Why ABA exists

ABA brings African manufacturers, formulators, distributors and specialists together to improve the conditions for biological agriculture.

The challenge

African agriculture is at a crossroads.

The problem

Biological products are entering markets through regulatory systems that were not designed for them.

Requirements vary, market access is uneven, and African manufacturers and formulators often have limited influence in the policy, regulatory and market forums that shape the sector.

National associations remain essential. ABA addresses a different problem: biological manufacturers increasingly work across African borders, while regulation and market access remain fragmented. Useful registration experience is scattered, local capability is less visible, and there is no shared, reviewed source of regulatory information across countries.

ABA's response

The sector must act together.

ABA gives biologicals organisations a way to identify shared barriers, agree priorities and take evidence into regulatory, policy and market discussions.

The Registration Tracker gathers structured updates on South African Act 36 biological-product applications that have already been submitted. Members and non-members can contribute, while public insights keep organisations and products private.

It also connects technical knowledge, supports clearer information about biological products and gives members a direct role in the work the alliance takes forward. ABA works with integrated pest management and farmer choice in mind, and stands for product quality and evidence.

What ABA does

Five pillars of work.

  1. 01

    Advocacy and representation

    Develop positions through documented consultation with members and technical specialists, and take them into policy, regulatory and market discussions.

  2. 02

    Enabling environment and harmonisation

    Use registration experience and shared evidence to support proportionate, science-based, risk-appropriate regulation, harmonised across African markets where the science justifies it, with less unnecessary duplication.

  3. 03

    Local manufacturing and circularity

    Make locally rooted production, formulation, research, skills and jobs more visible in the case for biological agriculture.

  4. 04

    Membership, chapters and governance

    Build a member alliance with clear participation rules and governance, and shape work in each country with local participants.

  5. 05

    Product clarity and sector credibility

    Develop clearer, reviewed information about biological products and the evidence behind them, and support action against fraudulent products. Membership does not mean endorsement.

What the work is meant to change

A credible evidence base

Agreed priorities and reviewed evidence give ABA a stronger basis for collective representation.

Clearer pathways

Registration experience can show where requirements are unclear, inconsistent or out of proportion to the risk.

Stronger African participation

Local organisations, technical specialists and researchers can shape the sector's development from within Africa.

The principle

ABA is building a stronger biologicals sector in Africa.

Where ABA works

Open across Africa.

ABA's founding members and current regulatory work are based in South Africa. Manufacturers, distributors, researchers and specialists elsewhere in Africa can express interest and contribute.
