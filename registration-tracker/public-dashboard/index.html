Registration insights.

ABA is collecting and reviewing information about South African Act 36 new registrations. Findings will be published only when the reviewed evidence is sufficient and can be combined without naming participants, organisations, or products.

Evidence status: collecting and reviewing. The views below show what ABA will publish when the evidence is sufficient; they contain no current totals or findings.

What this page will reveal

Four views will reveal shared registration patterns.

Together, they will show where registrations are held up, how long movement takes, which barriers recur, and where the evidence is strong enough to support a finding.

01

Process position

Where are new registrations waiting?

A stage-by-stage distribution will make concentrations and potential bottlenecks visible across included registrations.

02

Elapsed time

How does time compare with the published timeframe?

Combined elapsed time will be read against the applicable published timeframe, without exposing an individual registration.

03

Recurring barriers

Which obstacles appear across registrations?

Reviewed issues will be grouped consistently so recurring obstacles can be distinguished from isolated experiences.

What can ABA responsibly say?

A coverage map will show where comparisons are supported and where gaps limit what can responsibly be said.

Publication pipeline

Every published finding must pass the same evidence gates.

Submission starts a review process. It does not make information public or guarantee inclusion in a future finding.

  1. SubmittedA participant shares one registration.
  2. ReviewedABA checks quality and may request clarification.
  3. ClassifiedApproved information is mapped consistently.
  4. ProtectedNames and identifying detail stay out of the public view.
  5. PublishableThe combined evidence is sufficient and interpretable.
