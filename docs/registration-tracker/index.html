Registration tracker concept

A practical way for ABA to turn registration friction into member value and sector evidence.

The tracker is the bridge between product submissions, verified evidence, member support, and public advocacy. It starts with companies sharing structured registration data, then ABA reviews it before anything becomes visible in dashboards or registrar packets.

This is not just a form or a dashboard.

It is a controlled evidence system: public enough to support advocacy, private enough to earn company trust, and structured enough to become a real ABA member benefit.

Why ABA needs it

The tracker makes participation useful before the full platform exists.

Member value

Companies get a clearer view of their own registration work.

Members can see record status, blockers, benchmark context, and registrar packet readiness without exposing private company detail to the public.

Advocacy value

ABA gets a stronger evidence base for sector conversations.

Reviewed records can become anonymised public signals about backlog, wait time, bottlenecks, and product categories.

Operational value

Staff can separate raw submissions from trusted records.

Operator review controls verification, consent, duplicate handling, public aggregate use, and registrar packet inclusion.

Trust and rulebook summary

The tracker is useful because it is selective about what becomes visible.

This is the deeper rulebook summary to use if the room asks how ABA protects companies while still building a credible evidence base.

Raw intake is not public.

Submitted records first enter ABA review. They do not automatically feed the public dashboard or registrar packet.

ABA review creates trusted records.

Operators verify proof, membership relationship, references, duplicates, bottleneck themes, and clarification needs.

Consent controls visibility.

Internal review, public aggregate use, and named registrar packet use are separate consent boundaries.

Public dashboards are aggregate.

Only reviewed eligible records appear, and small cells remain suppressed to avoid revealing contributors.

Registrar packets have a stricter default.

Default candidates are reviewed, consented, operator-approved, verified Full member records with proof and reference status handled.

Company dashboards stay private.

A company sees its own records only, plus benchmark context where comparison is safe and meaningful.
