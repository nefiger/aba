Why ABA needs the picture

The tracker turns registration pain into patterns ABA can act on.

Country context

Where activity and blockage are visible.

Country-level grouping helps ABA see where records are concentrated, where visibility is thin, and where future coordination may be needed.

Time in stage

How long delays are lasting.

Measuring time in stage turns delay from anecdote into a comparable signal that can support more credible sector conversations.

Recurring bottlenecks

Which issues keep appearing.

Common themes such as categorisation uncertainty, submission friction, response delays, or duplicated trial requirements can be clustered into repeat patterns.

Africa-wide picture

South Africa is active now, but the record structure is built for a broader African registration picture.

Early intelligence may come from South African records first. As more contributors add product journeys, the same structure can reveal country-level demand, bottlenecks, and chapter readiness across the continent.

Trust boundaries

The tracker works because raw detail is reviewed before it is used.

ABA can build stronger evidence without making private company submissions public or using records outside the permissions attached to them.

Raw intake stays controlled.

Submitted records enter ABA review first. They do not automatically feed public dashboards or member workspaces.

Consent separates uses.

Internal review, member follow-up, and public aggregate use should remain separate permission decisions.

Public evidence is aggregate.

Public-facing signals should be anonymised, reviewed, and suppressed where small cells could reveal contributors.
