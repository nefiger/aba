Registration tracker meeting brief

A trusted picture of the registrations members have lodged with the Registrar's office.

The registration tracker gives ABA a detailed, reviewed view of member product and application records. From that record-level picture, ABA can support members, advocate with the Registrar's office, and publish anonymised sector signals without exposing private company detail.

Presenter framing

The feature is the registration picture, not just the dashboard.

Each submitted record helps ABA understand what members have lodged, where applications stand, what is blocking progress, and what can be used for member-specific or sector-wide advocacy.

Why ABA needs the picture

The tracker makes registration reality visible enough to act on.

ABA intelligence

A detailed view of member registrations.

ABA can see which product/application records members have lodged, where they sit, and which themes are emerging across the member base.

Member service

A practical reason to contribute data.

Members get a clearer private view of their own registration pipeline, blockers, readiness, and case-support opportunities.

Sector evidence

A missing evidence base for biologicals.

Anonymised patterns can become industry-wide signals about backlog, timelines, bottlenecks, and categories without revealing company records.

Normalization layer

The value comes from making records comparable.

ABA should group trusted records by country, registration stage, biological category, bottleneck theme, and time stuck. That middle layer turns member cases into sector intelligence without losing the case-level detail needed for member support.

Geographic truth

South Africa is active now, but the tracker should be built for an Africa-wide picture.

The first useful registration intelligence may come from South African member records, while the same record structure can later reveal country-level demand, bottlenecks, and chapter readiness across the continent.

Registration record lifecycle

One lodged application becomes a trusted record, then supports the right outputs.

1

Lodged application

A member has a product/application registration lodged with the Registrar's office.

2

Structured record

ABA captures the status, reference, category, evidence, bottleneck, consent, and next action.

3

ABA review

Operators check proof, membership relationship, duplicates, consent, and data quality.

4

Trusted record

The reviewed record becomes part of ABA's controlled registration picture.

5

Useful outputs

Trusted records can support member dashboards, Registrar advocacy, internal operations, and public aggregate signals.

What the tracker makes legible

Trusted records reveal patterns that individual registration stories cannot show alone.

Country picture

Where activity and blockage are showing up.

Country-level grouping helps ABA see where member records are concentrated and where future coordination may be needed.

Time stuck

How long delays are lasting.

Measuring time in stage turns delay from anecdote into a comparable signal that can support more credible conversations.

Recurring bottlenecks

Which issues keep appearing.

Common themes such as categorisation uncertainty, submission friction, or response delays can be clustered into repeat patterns.

Trust boundaries

The tracker works because raw detail is reviewed before it is used.

This is the short reassurance layer for the room: ABA can build evidence without making private company submissions public.

Raw intake stays controlled.

Submitted records enter ABA review first. They do not automatically feed public dashboards, member workspaces, or Registrar packets.

Consent separates uses.

Internal review, public aggregate use, and named Registrar-facing support should remain separate permission decisions.

Public evidence is aggregate.

Public-facing signals should be anonymised, reviewed, and suppressed where small cells could reveal contributors.
