Why ABA now

The sector needs more than products. It needs coordinated representation.

ABA exists to change the conditions under which African biologicals are seen, assessed, supported, and able to compete.

Independent African biologicals businesses are still navigating policy, registration, and visibility with too little coordinated support.

ABA turns isolated regulatory effort into shared evidence, a stronger collective voice, and clearer market-access pathways for credible biologicals across the continent.

Representation is thin.

Individual firms are left to navigate policy and market access alone, without a recognised collective vehicle for influence.

Registration journeys are opaque.

It is hard to see shared bottlenecks, recurring delays, and where evidence can strengthen engagement with regulators.

Local alternatives are harder to find.

Products, evidence, and regulatory context remain fragmented, making credible biologicals less visible than they should be.

The sector lacks a shared posture.

ABA helps turn scattered effort into a more coherent, legitimate, and Africa-rooted presence.

Three entry points into the ABA system.

These are not one funnel. Membership builds collective representation, the tracker turns lived registration work into shared evidence, and the explorer creates a more usable public knowledge layer.
