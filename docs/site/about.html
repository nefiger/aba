About ABA

ABA exists to strengthen how biologicals are understood, represented, and advanced across Africa.

The African Biologicals Alliance is being built as a coordinating platform for the biologicals sector: a place where industry participants, ecosystem contributors, and institutional stakeholders can engage through a clearer shared structure.

In practical terms

ABA is intended to be useful, not simply symbolic.

It should help make the sector more visible, create a stronger basis for participation and representation, and support a more credible public understanding of where biologicals are developing and where friction remains.

Role

A sector platform

ABA is not meant to be only a member list. It is meant to become a platform that links participation, knowledge, and evidence into a more coherent sector story.

Need

A clearer collective voice

Biologicals are often discussed with optimism, but the sector still needs better visibility, stronger coordination, and a more grounded basis for advocacy and engagement.

Scope

Across Africa

ABA is being designed with a continental horizon, while building from current operational activity in South Africa and growing wider participation over time.

What ABA Is Building Toward

A stronger structure for participation, visibility, and action.

Membership with clearer fit

Different organisations need different entry routes into ABA, and those relationships should be structured intentionally.

Better sector visibility

A stronger picture of products, organisations, country participation, and recurring sector patterns.

Public knowledge assets

A biologicals-first knowledge layer that helps the category feel legible, credible, and practically useful.

Stronger evidence for engagement

Over time, better visibility can support more informed conversations around regulation, market access, and sector development.

Who ABA Works With

ABA is being designed to work with core industry participants such as manufacturers, importers, distributors, and product owners; ecosystem contributors such as researchers, technical partners, and programme actors; and institutional stakeholders such as funders, development partners, and public-sector bodies.
