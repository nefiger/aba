Membership Types

Which option is right for you?

For industry participants

Full Member

Full membership is intended for organisations working directly in the biologicals market, including manufacturers, product owners, importers, and distributors.

Best if your commercial work is directly tied to biological products
Read full details
+

Full membership is for organisations whose day-to-day work is directly tied to biological products and the market around them. This may include organisations with products already in market, organisations moving products through registration, or organisations preparing to commercialise biologicals more actively.

This may be right for you if
  • Your organisation manufactures, imports, distributes, or owns biological products
  • Your products are already in market, in registration, or moving toward registration
  • You want ABA to understand your commercial position, regulatory context, and member priorities clearly
What full membership is designed to do

Full membership is for organisations that want a formal industry relationship with ABA. It gives ABA a clearer line of sight into where products are being built, moved, registered, and commercialised, so member needs can be surfaced with more precision and followed up more usefully.

What value this relationship is meant to create

The value is not abstract membership for its own sake. It is a stronger route into sector visibility, a more credible basis for representation, and a better starting point when ABA is identifying recurring registration or market-access friction affecting members.

For ecosystem contributors

Associate Member

Associate membership is intended for organisations contributing to the sector through research, technical work, programmes, implementation, or specialist support.

Best if you strengthen the sector without joining as a commercial industry member
Read full details
+

Associate membership is for organisations that help strengthen the biologicals sector without sitting in the same position as core industry participants. This may include research institutions, NGOs, technical partners, service organisations, and aligned programmes.

This may be right for you if
  • Your organisation contributes research, technical support, programme work, training, or specialist capacity
  • You want to participate in sector-building work without being placed in the core commercial lane
  • Your relationship with ABA is likely to centre on knowledge, implementation, capability, or collaboration
What associate membership is designed to do

Associate membership creates a formal place for the wider ecosystem around biologicals. It helps ABA stay connected to the organisations that generate evidence, deliver programmes, support adoption, build skills, and contribute specialist knowledge that the sector needs.

What value this relationship is meant to create

This lane gives your organisation a more defined way to participate in sector conversations, share expertise, and stay aligned with industry realities without claiming the same role as a manufacturer, importer, distributor, or product owner.

For institutional stakeholders

Observer

Observer status is intended for public institutions, funders, development partners, and other strategic stakeholders who want a formal relationship with ABA.

Best if your role is strategic or institutional rather than operational
Read full details
+

Observer status is for institutions that should be in formal relationship with ABA, but are not joining in the same way as industry or ecosystem members. This may include government stakeholders, development institutions, and funding partners.

This may be right for you if
  • You represent a public institution, funder, development partner, or similar strategic stakeholder
  • You want a formal route into ABA without entering the same membership path as industry or ecosystem participants
  • Your relationship with ABA is likely to focus on policy, coordination, public mandate, or strategic alignment
What observer status is designed to do

Observer status provides a structured way to stay connected to ABA’s work while preserving a clear distinction between institutional stakeholders and participating members. It recognises that some relationships matter strategically even when they are not membership relationships in the operational sense.

What value this relationship is meant to create

This route is meant to support more informed engagement, better coordination, and clearer lines of communication between ABA and the institutions that influence the sector environment around biologicals.

If you are applying as a manufacturer, importer, distributor, or product owner, start with Full Membership. If your organisation primarily supports the sector through research, implementation, technical work, or specialist capacity, Associate Membership is usually the better fit. If you represent a public institution, development partner, funder, or similar stakeholder, Observer status is likely to be the right place to begin.

What Membership Should Do

What an ABA relationship is meant to make possible.

A more accurate starting point

Your application helps ABA understand who you are, where you sit in the biologicals landscape, and what kind of follow-up or relationship actually makes sense for your organisation.

Stronger sector visibility

Every application contributes to a clearer picture of who is active in biologicals, where activity is happening, and which products, organisations, and institutional relationships shape the sector.

More useful follow-up

ABA should not come back to you with a generic response. The application is there to make the next conversation more relevant, whether that is membership follow-up, technical discussion, ecosystem alignment, or institutional engagement.

Better evidence for action over time

As participation grows, ABA is better placed to identify recurring pain points, show where friction is happening, and build a more credible basis for coordination, representation, and practical sector support.

ABA membership is not a guarantee of approvals, market outcomes, or institutional access. It is a structured starting point for a clearer relationship, better sector visibility, and more grounded follow-up.
