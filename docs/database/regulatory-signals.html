Recurring patterns

Bottlenecks and movements that appear across markets.

These are patterns that emerge from reading multiple country records together — not from any single source.

Frequent bottleneck

Dossier equivalence gaps

Most markets require full chemistry-equivalent dossiers for biological products, even where mode of action and risk profile differ significantly. This appears repeatedly as a primary delay source.

Frequent bottleneck

Data exclusivity barriers for SMEs

Smaller biological producers face disproportionate data-generation burdens without access to mutual recognition frameworks that larger registrants can leverage.

Watch pattern

Category definition ambiguity

Biostimulants are regulated as fertilisers in some markets and pesticides in others. This inconsistency creates registration uncertainty even for products with clear efficacy profiles.

Watch pattern

Shelf-life and formulation standards

Microbial product shelf-life requirements often reflect chemical pesticide norms. Cold-chain and viability standards are not consistently defined for live-organism products.

Positive movement

Mutual recognition interest growing

Several markets have signalled openness to regional registration harmonisation. COMESA and ECOWAS frameworks are beginning to reference biological-specific pathways in draft revisions.

Positive movement

Inoculant pathways becoming clearer

Rhizobium and mycorrhizal products are gaining clearer regulatory classification in South Africa and Kenya, which may create a reference pathway for other markets to follow.

Data gap

Semiochemical registration largely invisible

Pheromone and attractant products appear in very few country-level records. It is unclear whether this reflects limited use or limited registration pathway awareness.

Data gap

West Africa largely unmapped

Despite significant crop production in Nigeria, Côte d'Ivoire, and Senegal, biologicals registration records from these markets are sparse in current publicly accessible sources.

Watch pattern

Trial data localisation requirements

Multiple markets require local trial data for registration, even for products registered elsewhere on the continent. This creates duplication costs without proportionate risk reduction.
