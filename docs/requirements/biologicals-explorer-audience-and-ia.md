# Biologicals Explorer Audience And IA Notes

This note captures the current thinking behind the ABA knowledge hub direction before the crop-specific work continues.

## Core Reframe

The public-facing database should not behave like a technical repository or a long-form explainer page.

It should behave like a biologicals explorer:

- searchable
- browsable
- useful to non-specialists
- credible to technical and institutional audiences

The job of the homepage is not to explain everything.

Its job is to:

- show visitors what kind of information exists
- help them choose the right entry point
- make the category feel structured and legible
- create confidence that ABA is building something useful

## Main Audiences

### 1. Potential and current ABA members

They want to know:

- what kinds of biological products and categories are visible
- where their organisation or products fit
- whether ABA is building something credible and useful
- whether the sector feels organised enough to join

### 2. Sector professionals and technical contributors

They want to know:

- what products, crops, and pressures are represented
- what evidence exists
- how categories are being structured
- where they can contribute expertise or find related material

### 3. Institutional stakeholders

This includes development partners, public-interest bodies, donors, and policy-adjacent stakeholders.

They want to know:

- whether the sector is real, legible, and active
- where key patterns and gaps sit
- why ABA is a credible convening point
- whether the information base is strong enough to support engagement

### 4. Curious public and adjacent ecosystem audiences

They want to know:

- what biologicals are
- what they are used for
- how they connect to crops and pest pressures
- whether there is evidence and structure behind the category

## Real Entry Behaviours

Visitors are unlikely to arrive thinking “I want to browse a database.”

They are more likely to arrive with one of these starting points:

### Start from a product

Example question:
- what biological options exist in this category?

### Start from a crop

Example question:
- what biological options are relevant to citrus or maize?

### Start from a pressure

Example question:
- what biological options relate to fall armyworm or wheat rust?

### Start from proof

Example question:
- what evidence or trials support these options?

### Start from the wider landscape

Example question:
- what does the broader public and regulatory picture look like?

## Primary Versus Secondary Entry Modes

### Primary entry modes

These should dominate the homepage:

- Products
- Crops
- Pests & Diseases

Why:

- they are concrete
- they match real-world questions
- they support both search and browsing
- they are easier for general audiences to understand

### Secondary entry modes

These should still be present, but de-emphasised:

- Evidence
- Sector Signals

Why:

- they are important supporting layers
- they deepen credibility
- they matter more once a visitor has already found a product, crop, or pressure context

## Language Direction

Preferred framing:

- Biologicals Explorer
- Explore Biologicals
- Products, Crops, Pests & Diseases, Evidence, Sector Signals

Avoid leading with:

- database
- repository
- regulatory-first framing
- internal strategy language
- system or architecture explanations

## Homepage Job

The explorer homepage should answer:

- What can I find here?
- How do I want to start?
- Can I search directly?
- Is this substantial enough to trust?

It should not try to:

- explain the full ABA strategy
- narrate internal product logic
- foreground deep tracker or admin logic
- overload the visitor with too many parallel concepts

## Current Design Implication

The explorer landing page should:

- open with a concise explanation
- show quick summary stats
- provide one obvious search field
- foreground the three main browse modes
- keep Evidence and Sector Signals visible but secondary
- feel like an information product, not a concept note
