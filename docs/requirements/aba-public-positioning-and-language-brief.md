## 1. The proposition

Central line, used as the home page opening and the basis for everything else:

Building the regulatory and commercial environment for biological agriculture across Africa.

Supporting statement (the one place the positioning is spelled out in full):

ABA is pan-African evidence, regulatory intelligence and industry representation for biological agriculture. National associations remain essential. ABA works on what they cannot do alone: market access across borders and regulatory systems that differ from country to country.

What ABA stands for, in the language we build on (Jen's "gold" list, as public statements of position):

- proportionate, science-based, risk-appropriate regulation;
- product quality and efficacy standards;
- traceability, and enforcement against fraudulent products;
- stewardship and biosafety;
- regulator capacity;
- harmonisation where it is scientifically justified, less unnecessary duplication, and mutual reliance where appropriate;
- African manufacturing;
- farmers' access to useful biological innovation.

Framing: integrated pest management, farmer choice, innovation, evidence and quality. ABA is pro-evidence and pro-enforcement against bad products. It is not anti-chemical and not ideological.

Order on the page: proposition first, then evidence and the tracker, then membership. "Join ABA" is a secondary action, not the headline.

## 3. Claims we can and cannot make today

Can say now:

- ABA brings together African manufacturers, formulators, distributors and specialists.
- Founding members are setting ABA's first priorities.
- Expressions of interest are open. They do not confer membership. Admission is reviewed.
- The Technical Network is recruiting people who share ABA's purpose and standards.
- The Registration Tracker collects South African Act 36 applications that have already been submitted. Findings are grouped and never name people, organisations or products.
- Members' organisation names are listed only with their consent. Contact details are never listed.
- Membership is not endorsement of a member's products.

Cannot say now:

| Claim | Why not |
|---|---|
| Any number of countries, markets or members, or "members across Africa" | Not true today |
| "Represents the industry" or "the voice of African biologicals" | No mandate or recognition yet. Say what ABA brings together and works on |
| Any relationship with, recognition by, or participation in SADC, AU, FAO, COMESA or a named regulator | Unconfirmed. Do not name institutions as partners or targets |
| Any mention of SABO, CropLife, FERTASA or another association, by name or by implication ("the old industry", "breakaway") | Strategy rule. Competitor positioning is internal |
| Easier, faster or simpler registration, or "removing barriers" as an outcome | Reads as deregulation, and the guardrails bar faster-approval claims |
| "Members decide", "members vote", "members agree ABA's positions" | Voting rights and governance are not settled. Describe participation and consultation instead |
| An African regulatory map, annual report or verified database exists | Not yet published |
| "Independent" or "African-owned" as an eligibility rule | Not a settled decision |
| Any verified, official or complete data | Only after review and publication |

## 4. Wording: do and do not

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| Easier registrations; cut the red tape; barriers to biologicals | Proportionate, science-based, risk-appropriate regulation of biological products |
| Faster approval | Useful innovation reaching farmers. State it as the aim of good regulation, never as something ABA delivers |
| More suitable regulation (vague, and open to reading as weaker) | Regulation that fits the risk and the evidence |
| Join ABA (as a headline) | The proposition. Membership sits underneath as an action |
| ABA's database | African biologicals regulatory intelligence |
| South African association; starting in South Africa; Africa-wide ambition | Pan-African, stated as what ABA works on. See conflict 1 |
| Against chemicals; replacing chemical inputs | Integrated pest management and farmer choice |
| Better than, instead of, or replacing national associations | National associations remain essential. ABA addresses cross-border market access and regulatory fragmentation |
| Biologicals industry needs a voice | Evidence, quality and enforcement against fraudulent products |
| We represent / we speak for | ABA brings together; ABA publishes evidence; ABA takes agreed positions to regulators |

Standing rules from the guardrails stay in force: British and South African spelling, no em dashes, no bold in body copy, no heading max-width, no meta narration or prototype framing, no institutional or legal-protection copy on marketing pages, no rule-of-three lists, none of the banned words (ambition, ecosystem, route, operating system and the rest).

## 5. Registration Tracker framing

Name it as African biologicals regulatory intelligence infrastructure, not "ABA's database".

What the public page can show, and what each element must do:

- Provenance on every data point, with four labels: official regulator, manufacturer verified, ABA researched, unverified.
- A "last verified" date on every jurisdiction.
- A published methodology page: sources, definitions and update dates. This sits on the tracker methodology and privacy pages, not as a protective block on the landing page (trust job, not liability).
- A correction route, including for regulators to correct their own country's information.
- A structural separation, stated once and plainly: public regulatory data, confidential member data, and commercial leads are kept apart. Member and submission data never becomes a sales lead. Anyone who wants commercial contact opts in, separately.

Staged state for launch. The module shows "Insufficient data" until reviewed data is published. Working wording: "Insufficient data. Findings appear here once enough reviewed submissions are available." The page does not describe a rollout, a threshold or a pipeline, and the site going live is not reported as the tracker being live.

Scope stays factual. The tracker today covers South African Act 36 applications. Say that where it is the fact (what you can submit). Do not use it as the headline positioning of the tracker, and do not imply coverage of other countries.

## 6. Conflicts and decisions for Jen

I have not weakened any guardrail. Each of these needs your call.

1. South Africa. The strategy says do not headline South Africa. AGENTS.md says preserve "Africa-wide participation, South Africa currently active", and the guardrails' accepted example is "ABA is based in South Africa and welcomes participation from across Africa". Recommendation: no South Africa in headlines or the home hero. Keep one plain factual sentence on About (where ABA's founding members and current work are) because the no-unearned-claims rule requires honesty about scope, and keep the factual scope on the tracker. AGENTS.md would then need a one-line update.
2. "Faster access to useful innovation" is on the strategy's supportive list, but the guardrails bar faster-approval claims. Recommendation: allowed only as an aim of proportionate regulation, never as an outcome ABA delivers.
3. "Members decide" and "Members agree" in the home page cycle describe governance that is not decided, and conflict with the anti-capture design in the strategy. Recommendation: rewrite as members contributing to priorities, with ABA publishing the positions it takes.
4. Correction route. The tracker needs one, and the site has no contact route at all (HANDOVER 2026-08-03). Decision needed: which address or form carries tracker corrections. Without it we cannot promise one.
5. State-dependent copy cannot be "final" in the usual sense. Membership interest turning into open applications, the logo scaffold filling with consented names, and the tracker moving from "Insufficient data" to published findings will each change the page. Recommendation: write these as state blocks, so a state change swaps a block and the voice never changes. This honours the no-further-copy-changes decision.
6. The "Member logo / Pending" placeholders on home are prototype framing and expose unconsented members. Recommendation: remove until there are named, consented members.
7. "Infrastructure" and "intelligence" are not on the banned list, but sit close to the internal-model language the guardrails reject. They are your requested terms for the tracker, so I propose keeping them there and nowhere else, and I will check them against the preflight.
