# ABA Membership Value And Engagement Model Notes

Last updated: 2026-07-20

## Purpose

Set out the working answer to a foundational first-release question: if the registration tracker is useful to non-members, why should a person or organisation engage with ABA, and why should they eventually become a member?

This is a product and content decision note. It is not membership policy, a pricing schedule, or a public-facing page. It records the value proposition that the site must make legible before implementation, while keeping claims within what ABA can substantiate and deliver.

## Source basis

- the final ABA brochure and accompanying core-functions positioning diagram
- ABA UX workshop recording, 15 July 2026, especially the discussion at approximately 40:03, 43:24, 44:21, 44:48, and 1:08:53
- workshop transcript and the related founding-group discussion supplied with this reset
- the first-release website reset specification and terminology register

The brochure remains the authority for ABA's broad purpose and pillars. The workshop is the authority for the immediate engagement model and for the distinction between public tracker access and member-specific value.

## 1. The core answer

ABA should not make the registration tracker private simply to force membership. A person with a registration matter should be able to understand the tracker and, where eligible, submit a registration situation whether or not they are a member.

That public utility has two purposes:

1. it makes ABA useful to a wider part of the sector; and
2. it builds evidence that can support collective advocacy and a clearer picture of registration barriers.

Membership is therefore not the price of entry to basic tracker participation. It is the route into a more direct, ongoing, and participatory relationship with ABA: contributing to priorities, being represented through agreed collective processes, and being eligible for appropriate member-facing visibility and network activity as those services are established.

The public site must communicate this without creating a false binary of `member` versus `non-member`. The relevant progression is:

| Visitor situation | What ABA should make clear | Appropriate next step now |
|---|---|---|
| Curious visitor | What ABA is, why biological alternatives need a coherent sector voice, and what ABA is working to change | Explore ABA's purpose and work |
| Interested stakeholder | Whether ABA is relevant to their organisation, work, or interest in the sector | Receive ABA updates / register membership interest |
| Registration practitioner | Whether the tracker is relevant to a real registration situation | Understand or begin permitted tracker intake |
| Invited applicant | What ABA needs to assess a prospective membership relationship | Complete the initial membership intake form through the private URL |
| Member | How to participate in ABA's collective work and use the member-facing opportunities that are actually available | Engage through the approved membership relationship |

The first release should help each person find the appropriate path. It must not make membership interest look like an application, or make tracker participation look like membership.

## 2. Four layers of value

### 2.1 Why ABA exists: the sector proposition

The brochure's five pillars explain ABA's reason for being:

- advocacy and representation
- enabling environment and harmonisation
- local manufacturing and circularity
- membership, chapters, and governance
- product clarity and sector credibility

This is the public, values-led story: ABA is an African sector body working towards fairer market conditions, stronger regulatory pathways, and a coherent African voice for biological alternatives. It is not a promise that every pillar is already a finished service.

### 2.2 Why engage: an appropriate route for each need

The site should provide useful routes without making every visitor join immediately:

- Track a registration matter where the tracker is relevant.
- Receive updates where a visitor wants to stay informed about ABA and future membership opening.
- Make contact through the technical-network route only if the purpose, response expectation, and availability are clear.
- Explore membership when it is appropriate, with the public release framed as interest rather than application.

This is not a reduced membership story. It makes ABA credible: the action presented should match what the person actually needs and what ABA can do now.

### 2.3 Why become a member: a more direct collective relationship

The workshop identified the following value areas. Each needs a defined eligibility rule, consent boundary, and operational owner before it is promised as a live service.

#### A. Structured regulatory representation

At approximately 40:03, the workshop discussed compiling a specific list of member registration matters for ABA's engagement with the registrar. At approximately 1:08:53, that registrar-facing use was discussed as available for members only.

The defensible proposition is not individual access to a regulator, faster approval, or a guaranteed regulatory result. It is:

> Eligible members may be able to have relevant registration matters represented through ABA's structured engagement with the registrar, subject to member authorisation, ABA review, and the agreed engagement process.

Public copy must not say:

- `direct access to the registrar`
- `ABA will resolve your registration`
- `membership accelerates approval`
- `regulatory representation` without explaining the collective and conditional nature of the activity

The exact public wording should be finalised only after the authorisation and inclusion process is agreed.

#### B. Member product visibility

At approximately 43:24, the workshop distinguished between public information about ingredients or functions and named member product information. The relevant principle is that a public knowledge layer can explain biologicals generally, while named member products need an explicit, member-linked publication decision.

The defensible proposition is:

> Where ABA has approved the relevant information and channel, eligible member products can be considered for named visibility in ABA's product-information activity.

This must not imply automatic listing, endorsement, registration approval, comparative claims, or a guaranteed marketing outcome. It also does not mean that all product information is member-only; public education about biological alternatives can remain public.

#### C. Technical network and product-information channels

At approximately 44:21–44:42, the workshop discussed making relevant product information available to agronomists and a technical network, supporting familiarity and appropriate understanding.

The defensible proposition is:

> ABA is developing technical-network and product-information channels that can help relevant, reviewed member information reach appropriate technical audiences.

Avoid promises of referrals, individual recommendations, commercial leads, technical advice, or endorsement. Whether the Technical Network is in the first release is a separate scope decision.

#### D. Market development and communications

At approximately 44:48–45:13, the workshop discussed the possibility of product sharing, videos, and communications reaching farmers and agronomists. This is a future-facing capability, not a standard package to promise today.

The defensible proposition is:

> ABA is building channels that can support accurate sector and product communication to relevant agricultural audiences over time.

Do not promise a marketing package, sales generation, media placement, or access to a defined audience until an operational offer exists.

#### E. Influence, participation, and governance

This is the most durable membership value: members contribute to an organised sector voice, help identify barriers and priorities, and participate in the collective direction of the alliance through the governance arrangements ABA establishes.

Public copy can say that membership is a way to help shape ABA's priorities and collective advocacy. It must not promise voting rights, committee seats, chapter roles, or governance powers until the membership policy specifies who has them.

#### F. Developing resources and relationships

Members may eventually have more structured access to ABA resources, sector learning, technical connections, and product-information opportunities. These should be presented as developing work or future availability, not as an undifferentiated benefit list.

## 3. Public tracker and membership: a clear boundary

| Topic | Public tracker participation | Membership |
|---|---|---|
| Basic purpose | Capture and understand a real registration situation; build permitted evidence for broader advocacy | Build an ongoing, approved relationship with ABA and participate in its collective work |
| Availability in first release | Public orientation; permitted intake where the tracker workstream allows it | Private initial intake only; public site captures interest |
| Required relationship | Not a membership application or activation path | A reviewed and activated membership relationship |
| Data use | Participant-provided information, handled according to tracker consent and privacy rules | Membership, organisation, participation, and any separately authorised member activity |
| Regulatory engagement | May inform aggregate understanding of barriers | May support structured inclusion of relevant authorised member matters in ABA's engagement, once process is agreed |
| Product visibility | No implication that tracker submission publishes a product | Possible eligible member-facing opportunity, subject to review and policy |

The tracker can create a legitimate later conversion opportunity: a non-member who finds it useful may decide to register membership interest. That invitation must be subtle and truthful, for example: `If you would also like to hear as ABA opens membership, receive ABA updates.` It must never make submission conditional on joining.

## 4. Relationship and consent model

The public interest route should create a CRM contact relationship, not a member or membership application by default.

For internal continuity, ABA may hold one person/contact record and one organisation record where applicable. Separate relationships then record what the person has actually done or requested:

- public interest in ABA or membership opening
- permission to receive ABA updates
- tracker participation
- membership application
- approved membership relationship
- technical-network relationship, where applicable

This avoids duplicate identities while preserving the important distinctions. A person who receives updates is not a member; a tracker participant is not an applicant; an approved application is not yet active membership.

Use the internal term `Communication consent` for permission to send updates. Use `Communication preferences` only for optional topic/channel choices once ABA offers those choices. On public forms, use plain language such as `Receive ABA updates` and clear consent text rather than the internal words `subscription` or `contact subscription`.

## 5. Website implications

### Home

Lead with why ABA exists. Then offer progressively relevant routes: understand ABA, track a registration matter, receive ABA updates, and—where appropriate—learn why membership matters.

### Membership page

Do not begin with an application form. Explain the membership proposition in layers:

1. collective sector purpose;
2. current and developing participation/value areas;
3. what is available in this release; and
4. the public action: receive updates about future membership opening.

For invited people, the private route can explain the current intake and review process without confusing it with the public page.

### Registration tracker page

Make the tracker useful in its own right. Present a restrained, optional connection to ABA updates or membership interest after the tracker purpose and next action are clear.

### Technical Network and product-information work

Only make a public destination where there is a clear current action and response boundary. Otherwise, describe this as part of the developing ABA programme rather than creating an empty service route.

## 6. Claim discipline for the first release

| Use this framing | Do not use this framing |
|---|---|
| `ABA represents a collective sector voice` | `ABA gets your product registered` |
| `subject to authorisation and ABA review` | `all member cases are taken to the registrar` |
| `eligible member product information may be considered` | `members are listed and promoted` |
| `ABA is developing technical and market-development channels` | `members receive customers, leads, or endorsements` |
| `membership is being opened in phases` | `join now` when public application is unavailable |
| `receive ABA updates` | `subscribe` when the purpose is not clear |

## 7. Decisions still needed before hard-coded public claims

1. The eligibility, authorisation, review, and withdrawal rules for a member matter to be included in registrar-facing engagement.
2. The scope, review process, and publication channels for member product visibility.
3. Whether the Technical Network has a first-release route and what response ABA commits to provide.
4. Which member categories/types carry which participation and governance privileges.
5. The precise membership-interest fields, privacy text, and communication-consent wording.
6. The approved distinction between available member value and developing programme work.

Until these are resolved, the site should use the restrained language in this note and keep the relevant detail out of forms, navigation, and sales-style claims.
