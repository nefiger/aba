## 2. About

Source: `docs/site/about.html`

**Headline:** The biologicals sector in Africa needs an independent African Alliance.

Biological crop protection and biostimulants are growing across Africa — but the sector is fragmented, its voice is thin, and the regulatory environment was not designed with it in mind. ABA is the alliance that changes that: a membership body, a knowledge resource, and a trusted intermediary between what members know and what the sector needs to say.

South Africa is the current operating base, while the alliance is designed to support participation and sector relationship-building across the continent.

ABA is anchored in South Africa, where the regulatory framework, market activity, and founding membership are currently concentrated. The model is designed to extend across Africa as participation grows.

#### ABA is being built to give the sector a credible centre of gravity.

**The problem**

Companies working in biologicals are navigating registration pathways, market access questions, and credibility challenges without a shared structure to draw on. Each company carries the full weight alone.

**What ABA provides**

Membership, shared knowledge, structured registration intelligence, and a trusted review layer that turns what members know into what the sector can credibly say without exposing private company detail.

**The ambition**

A biologicals sector in Africa that is well-represented, easier to navigate, and taken seriously by the regulatory and policy environments it operates within.

#### Five things ABA does that the sector currently lacks.

**A trusted intermediary — not just a network**

ABA sits between what members know privately and what the sector can say publicly. That review layer is what makes shared intelligence usable — it protects sensitive detail while making the aggregate credible.

**Registration support for members**

Members can submit registration records to ABA. ABA structures them, tracks their status, and uses them to provide member-specific Registrar-office advocacy — the kind that requires organised, reviewed evidence.

**Sector-wide advocacy grounded in data**

Aggregated registration signals — delays, recurring bottlenecks, category-level patterns — give ABA something to say to regulators and policymakers that no individual company can say alone.

**A shared knowledge base for the category**

The Biologicals Explorer makes products, crop contexts, evidence records, MRLs, and regulatory standards accessible in one place — structured for the people who need to use them, not just store them.

**Membership that fits different roles**

Industry members, technical contributors, and institutional stakeholders each have a route into ABA. Structure is designed to be proportionate to role — not a single flat membership for everyone.

#### There are three useful next steps.

Membership is how you participate. The tracker shows how ABA turns submissions into advocacy intelligence. The Biologicals Explorer shows the shared knowledge layer in practice.

- _Button:_ Open the tracker
- _Button:_ Open Biologicals Explorer

## 5. Governance and Data

Source: `docs/site/governance-and-data.html`

**Headline:** How ABA handles privacy, participation, and member-facing obligations.

These pages outline the policy surfaces the prototype already needs: public privacy and POPIA handling, general site terms, member-specific use conditions, and route-specific data consent expectations.

ABA already captures membership, update, and registration-intelligence inputs. Formal legal drafting still needs board and counsel review, but the prototype now treats these policy areas as required product surfaces, not optional afterthoughts.

#### Privacy and POPIA notice

ABA needs a clear public notice covering personal and organisational information captured through membership, updates, and registration-intelligence routes.

- What information ABA collects
- Why ABA collects it
- How long it is kept
- Who inside ABA may access it
- How a person requests correction or removal where appropriate

#### Public website terms

The public site needs general terms for browsing, contributing information, and using ABA knowledge surfaces responsibly.

- Permitted use of the site and public resources
- No misuse of tracker or form submissions
- No implied regulatory or commercial guarantee from public information
- Appropriate attribution and reuse expectations

#### Member-specific conditions

Membership introduces additional obligations beyond ordinary public usage. These terms need to cover conduct, access, confidentiality, and member-only resources.

- Code of ethics and independence expectations
- Use of member-only materials and operational data
- Participation boundaries for technical partners and observers
- Grounds for review, suspension, or withdrawal where relevant

#### Route-specific data handling

The prototype already implies different consent expectations across membership, updates, and tracker routes. Those should be made explicit and legible.

- Membership review and follow-up consent
- Newsletter and update permissions
- Use of aggregate signals for advocacy and sector intelligence
- Links between membership records and future tracker submissions
